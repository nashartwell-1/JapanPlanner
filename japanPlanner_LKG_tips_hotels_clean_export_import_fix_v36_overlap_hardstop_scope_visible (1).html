Transit & IC Cards

  • IC cards (Suica/PASMO/ICOCA): Tap for trains/buses and at konbini; reload at station machines.
  • Shinkansen: Reserve busy routes (Tokyo ⇄ Kyoto/Osaka) via SmartEX or JR counters; arrive a few minutes early.
  • Transfers: Follow platform arrows/line names (JR/Metro). Trains are punctual.
  • Escalators: Stand left in Tokyo, right in Osaka; let riders exit first.

Cash, Payments & Connectivity

  • Cash: Small eateries, market stalls, and shrines may be cash‑only. 7‑Eleven/JP Post ATMs accept foreign cards.
  • Cards/contactless: Visa/Mastercard/AmEx widely accepted at chains; tap‑to‑pay common in cities.
  • Data: Use an eSIM or pocket Wi‑Fi (5–10 GB per traveler). Download offline maps and the Japanese pack in your translate app.

Dining & Reservations

  • Peak times: 12:00–13:30 & 18:30–20:00. Go early/late for short waits.
  • Booking tools: TableCheck / Omakase; for tough spots, ask your hotel concierge.
  • Menus: Many places have photo/plastic model menus. Point and say “これください (kore kudasai)”.
  • Allergies/diet: Dashi often contains fish; some sauces use mirin (alcohol). Ask staff if you avoid these.

Etiquette & Everyday Basics

  • Keep voices low on trains; phone calls discouraged. Use silent mode.
  • Remove shoes in homes and some restaurants—look for slippers.
  • Trash bins are scarce; carry a small bag and sort when you find bins.
  • Onsen: Rinse thoroughly before entering; tattoos may be restricted—check policies or book a private bath.

Shrines & Temples

  • Be respectful: avoid loud voices and flash; don’t block paths.
  • At chozuya (basin), rinse left hand, right hand, then mouth.

Shopping & Tax‑Free

  • Tax‑free: Bring your passport; minimum spend (often ¥5,000+ pre‑tax). Keep receipts until departure.
  • Compare: Bic Camera, Yodobashi, and Don Quijote often have tourist coupons—ask at the counter.
  • Ship it: Many stores can ship domestically/internationally for bulky purchases.

Luggage & Logistics

  • Takkyubin: Forward suitcases to the next hotel (next‑day) via hotel desk or konbini.
  • Coin lockers: Many stations have small/medium/large lockers; IC card or coins.
  • Smaller stations may lack elevators—pack so you can manage stairs.

Language & Tools

  • Key phrases: “すみません (sumimasen)”, “ありがとうございます (arigatō gozaimasu)”, “これください (kore kudasai)”.
  • Use translate camera; download the Japanese offline pack.
  • Save exact locations in Google Maps; addresses are often block‑based.

Safety & Emergencies

  • Japan is very safe; still use common sense with bags/phones.
  • Pharmacies (ドラッグストア) have basic meds; keep travel insurance details handy.
  • Emergency numbers: Police 110, Fire/Ambulance 119.
